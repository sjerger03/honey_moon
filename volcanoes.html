Volcano National Park

Kīlauea Summit & Eruption Viewing

Overlook

The eruption area itself is closed to entry, but when Kīlauea is active you can watch lava fountains from designated viewpoints around the caldera — best at dawn, dusk, or after dark. Episodes can pause for days or weeks, so lava isn't guaranteed. Check conditions the morning of your visit.

Kīlauea Iki Trail

Hike · 2–3 hrs

A 4-mile trail descending through rainforest onto the floor of a crater that was a lake of molten lava in 1959 — one of the park's most scenic hikes. Allow 2–3 hours.

Nāhuku — Thurston Lava Tube

Short walk

A short rainforest walk leads into a roughly 500-year-old lava tube, where lava once flowed at close to 2,000°F. Easy to pair with Kīlauea Iki right next door.

Steam Vents & Sulphur Banks

Roadside · 5–10 min

Quick pull-offs right in the summit area where groundwater seeping into hot volcanic rock rises back up as steam — no trail required. Sulphur Banks (Haʻakulamanu) adds mineral-stained, sulfur-smelling ground nearby. Barely a detour on the way to the next stop.

Chain of Craters Road

Scenic drive · Half day

A 19-mile drive from the summit down to the coast, through lava flows, craters, and rainforest, ending at Hōlei Sea Arch. No 4WD needed — allow at least half a day if you stop along the way.

It's a dead end — the road doesn't connect back to Puna, so you'll drive the same 19 miles back up to the summit afterward.

Mauna Ulu

Short walk

A dramatic, relatively young lava landscape from a 1969–1974 eruption. Short walks lead out onto the flow with views back toward the crater.

Puʻuloa Petroglyphs

Short walk

A short trail off Chain of Craters Road leads to one of Hawaiʻi's largest petroglyph fields — thousands of images carved into the lava by early Hawaiians. Stay on the boardwalk.

Beach

Kaimū Black Sand Beach

ActivityLava-buried beach + walk
From Kalani~15 min drive
Best TimeMorning or late afternoon
Duration30–60 min

The original Kaimū Beach and most of Kalapana were buried by a 1990 Kīlauea lava flow. The current black-sand shoreline sits on top of that lava field — reaching it is about a 10-minute, 300-meter walk across the flow, with coconut palms growing up through the rock along the way. The sand shifts as waves move it and can disappear and rebuild over time. Surf and currents are rough, so this isn't a swimming beach.
